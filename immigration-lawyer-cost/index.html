What raises the cost of your case

Our fee starts from the type of case. A history with the police or with immigration adds hours, and some situations add filings and government fees of their own:

A criminal record or a past arrest

has to be checked against the immigration rules before anything is filed, and a case with criminal charges in it takes more hours.

A past deportation

also takes more hours, and a past deportation or time in the United States without status (unlawful presence) can call for a waiver, a separate filing with its own fee: Form I-601 ($1,050), the provisional waiver on Form I-601A ($795), or permission to reapply after removal on Form I-212 ($1,175).

Immigration court

is billed by the hour, where most cases at USCIS have a flat fee.

Each extra family member

pays their own application fees, such as a separate green card application or visa fee.

An appeal

adds a filing: $800 for a USCIS appeal or motion on Form I-290B, and $1,060 to appeal an immigration judge's decision to the Board of Immigration Appeals.

Government fees by type of case

The amounts below come from the USCIS fee schedule (Form G-1055, edition of October 26, 2026), the State Department's visa fee list and the immigration court's fee pages, all read on November 16, 2026 (USCIS fee schedule). For many forms you can file online, and the online fee is then $50 lower than the paper fee.

Family petition (Form I-130)$675 on paper, $625 online
Green card application (Form I-485)$1,440 on paper, $1,390 online; $950 on paper for a child under 14 filing with a parent
Work permit while the green card case is pending (Form I-765)$260
Travel permit while it is pending (Form I-131, advance parole)$630 on paper, $580 online
Affidavit of support (Form I-864) and medical exam form (Form I-693)no USCIS fee

For a marriage green card, the petition and the green card application come to $2,115 on paper.

These are the court's fees, plus the DHS fingerprint fee, in a deportation defense case, from the court's forms and appeal fee pages.

Work visas and employer green cards

An employer's green card petition (Form I-140) costs $715 on paper plus a $600 Asylum Program Fee, which drops to $300 for a small employer or a self-petitioner. Most work visa petitions and employer green card petitions can get a faster USCIS response with premium processing, for $2,965 more ($1,780 for H-2B and R petitions). Each visa page lists its own fees: H-1B, O-1, EB-1, EB-2 NIW and EB-3 and PERM.

Costs besides the filing fees

Medical examfor a green card application in the United States, a civil surgeon does the exam and sets the price, and prices vary from one civil surgeon to the next (USCIS).
Translationsevery document in another language submitted to USCIS needs a full English translation, with the translator's statement that it is complete and accurate and that they are competent to translate (8 CFR 103.2).
Records and travelcertified copies of birth, marriage and court records, and the trip to your interview or, for a case abroad, to the consulate.

Fee waivers and reduced fees

USCIS waives its own fee only on certain forms, on Form I-912. The list includes green card renewal (I-90), removing conditions (I-751), most work permits (I-765, except DACA), naturalization (N-400) and certificates of citizenship (N-600). The family petition (I-130) is not on the list, and the green card application (I-485) qualifies only in a few categories, such as asylees (USCIS).

You can qualify in one of three ways (USCIS):

A means-tested benefit

that you, your spouse or your child receives now, or your parent if you are under 21 or disabled

Household income

at or below 150% of the federal poverty guidelines when you file

Extreme financial hardship,

such as unexpected medical bills or an emergency, that keeps you from paying the fee

For most forms a fee waiver request has to be filed on paper with the form itself. The fees Congress added in 2025, including the asylum fees, can't be waived.

Free and low-cost legal help

USCIS says you don't need a representative to file its forms. If you want help and can't pay a private lawyer, go to someone allowed to give legal advice: an attorney, or a representative accredited by the Department of Justice. A notary public in the United States is not a lawyer and cannot represent you (USCIS).

Non-profit organizations recognized by the Department of Justice represent people through their accredited representatives. Only non-profit, tax-exempt organizations can be recognized, and the program exists to bring representation to people with low incomes (EOIR). For people in removal proceedings, the immigration court hands out a list of non-profit organizations and attorneys who have committed to at least 50 hours a year of free work at that court, along with referral services; EOIR updates it every quarter (EOIR).

If hiring a private lawyer is out of reach, we encourage you to use these programs before you file alone.

USCIS keeps the fee if a case is denied

USCIS generally keeps the filing fee whatever the decision and however long the case takes; a refund is at its discretion (8 CFR 103.2). A denied application means paying the fees again and starting a new wait. Our work goes into avoiding that: at the consultation Zachary checks which route fits your case before you pay for a petition, he checks the evidence against what USCIS asks for on that form before it's filed, and he prepares you for the USCIS interview and can attend it with you.

Questions about immigration lawyer fees

How much does an immigration lawyer cost?

At Cruz Gold & Associates most green card, citizenship and family cases have one flat fee, quoted at the free consultation before you decide, and immigration court cases are billed by the hour. Government fees come on top, for example $2,115 on paper for a family petition and green card application filed together, or $760 for a citizenship application on paper.

Is the consultation free?

Yes. The consultation with attorney Zachary Gold is free and lasts 20 minutes, and you can book it online from any page of this site.

How much does an immigration lawyer charge per hour?

We don't publish an hourly rate. We bill by the hour only for immigration court work, and Zachary tells you the rate and the retainer at the consultation, before you hire us. USCIS cases such as green cards and citizenship usually have a flat fee.

Can I get a free immigration lawyer?

Sometimes. People in removal proceedings receive the immigration court's list of non-profits and attorneys who commit to free work at that court, and non-profits recognized by the Department of Justice, whose program aims to serve people with low incomes, can represent you through accredited representatives.

Are USCIS fees refundable?

Generally no. USCIS keeps the filing fee whatever the decision and however long the case takes, and any refund is at its discretion (8 CFR 103.2).

Do you offer payment plans?

We accept credit cards, and when money is tight we try to set up a payment plan. Ask about it at the consultation.

Do I need an immigration lawyer?

USCIS doesn't require one; you may file its forms yourself. A lawyer is worth the fee when a mistake would cost more: a past arrest or removal, a problem with your status, a deadline, or an interview you want help with. At the free consultation Zachary tells you whether your case needs a lawyer.
