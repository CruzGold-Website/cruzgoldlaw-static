USCIS translation checklist: prepare your documents

Written by Zachary Gold, Esq.Owner and lead attorney

Quick answer: For a foreign-language document submitted to USCIS, prepare the source document, a full English translation and the translator's certification that the translation is complete and accurate and that the translator is competent to translate into English. Check the specific form instructions for the required source record and whether to submit a copy or an original.

A translated birth certificate can still leave you with a question: do you have the right document, every page and the required certification? Use this checklist to sort those questions before filing. It covers documents submitted to USCIS; consulates and immigration courts have separate requirements that need their own review.

Start with the document your filing needs

Read the evidence instructions for your actual form before ordering a translation. A translation changes the language of a record. It does not establish that the record is the right kind of evidence for your application or petition.

For example, a translated extract and a translated full record may contain different information. Ask which source record your filing requires rather than assuming that any translated certificate will do. If you are gathering joint-petition marriage evidence, start with the separate I-751 evidence checklist; the checks here address the translation component.

Under 8 CFR 103.2(b)(3), foreign-language documents submitted to USCIS need full English translations with the required translator certifications. A few translated names and dates do not amount to a complete translation of a longer record.

Keep the three components together

Make a separate set for each record. This table is an organization aid, not an official USCIS packet format.

Component What to check before review
Foreign-language source A legible record, with all relevant pages and any writing on the reverse available for translation
English translation The complete translation corresponding to that source, rather than selected passages or a summary
Translator certification A statement covering completeness, accuracy and competence to translate from the source language into English

Keep an untouched copy of the source file. A cropped phone photograph may leave out a stamp, handwritten entry or page number. If something is unreadable, flag it for the translator instead of guessing the missing words yourself.

Check what the translator certifies

Before accepting the finished work, check that the certification addresses:

  • Completeness: The translation covers the document submitted for translation.
  • Accuracy: The translator certifies that the English version accurately translates the source.
  • Competence: The translator certifies competence to translate from the identified foreign language into English.

Follow the current form instructions for signatures and any identifying or contact details. Keep the certification clearly associated with the document it covers. A receipt for translation services or a statement that someone speaks English does not, by itself, address all these certification points.

If you are unsure who should translate a particular record or whether an existing certification is sufficient, ask before filing. This checklist does not decide whether you, a relative or a particular provider should do the work. Do not assume that a vendor's marketing promise guarantees acceptance of your evidence or approval of the case.

Compare the source and translation

You can check the set for obvious omissions even when you cannot assess every translated word. Put the versions side by side and note questions for the translator or your attorney:

  • Do the page numbers and document descriptions correspond?
  • Are names, dates and identification numbers visible, without cut-off edges?
  • Has text on the reverse, a stamp or a handwritten entry been overlooked?
  • Does the translation identify the same source record as the certification?

Different spellings or name formats deserve attention. List the differences and ask how to address them accurately. Do not edit an original record or tell a translator to make two documents match by changing what one actually says.

The USCIS Policy Manual's evidence chapter says a translator's summary is unacceptable and explains that USCIS may request original documents. Check your form's copy and original requirements separately from the translation requirement.

Prepare a review folder before filing

Label each set with the document type and the person's name, then make a short list of missing pages, unclear text and spelling differences. These labels help you organize your own records; they are not required USCIS headings. Save the final version reviewed for submission and keep the originals accessible.
