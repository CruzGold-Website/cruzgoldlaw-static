I-751 evidence checklist: documents to gather before filing

Cruz Gold & Associates · November 11, 2026

Quick answer: For a joint marriage-based I-751 petition, gather card copies and documents showing a good-faith marriage, including records of your shared home, finances and life from the wedding through the present. Additional documents depend on your circumstances, including children or criminal history; an individual filing or waiver needs a separate review of its requirements.

Start with the filing route and dates

Form I-751 removes the conditions on permanent residence obtained through marriage. It is different from renewing a standard 10-year green card. If you are unsure which card or process you have, review the distinction in our green card services before assembling a filing.

For the usual joint petition filed by spouses, USCIS requires filing during the 90 days immediately before conditional residence expires. Individual filings, waivers, certain children's petitions and military situations have different rules. If the deadline has passed or your spouse cannot file with you, get advice promptly rather than relying on the joint-filing checklist below.

Start with the current USCIS Form I-751 page. Its initial-evidence checklist is a preparation aid and does not replace the form instructions or legal requirements.

Your I-751 document checklist

Use this table to sort what you already have. The relationship records are examples, not a requirement to own a home, have children or maintain every kind of joint account. The evidence must reflect your actual marriage.

CategoryDocuments to gatherWhat to check
Conditional resident cardsLegible front-and-back copies of your card and the cards of any conditional resident children included in the petitionBoth sides are present; confirm whether a child can be included or needs a separate petition
Shared homeJoint leases, mortgage records, deeds or other records of living togetherNames, addresses and dates match your history; explain periods apart truthfully
Shared financesJoint bank statements with transaction history, complete joint federal and state tax returns, shared loans and utility billsRecords show the arrangements you actually used over time
Insurance and beneficiariesRelevant insurance policies naming your spouse as beneficiaryThe documents identify the people covered or named and the relevant dates
Children, if applicableBirth certificates of children born during the marriageThe record is legible; do not treat having children as a condition of approval
Other relationship evidenceRelevant photographs, travel records and detailed statements from people who know you bothExplain the context; do not rely on photographs or letters alone

The USCIS I-751 instructions describe the evidence categories in detail. Read the separate requirements for criminal records, children and your filing basis as well.

Show the history, not only the latest month

USCIS asks for evidence showing the circumstances of the relationship from the date of the marriage to the present for a joint petition. A folder containing only your wedding photographs or your newest bank statement leaves much of that history unexplained.

As an organization step, make a simple timeline: wedding, addresses, moves and any periods living apart. Compare it with the dates on your leases, accounts and other records. The petition also asks for residence and employer histories since you obtained conditional status.

This timeline is a checking aid, not an official USCIS form. Do not invent a document for a missing period or change an address to make the records look consistent. Identify the gap and discuss what genuine records or explanation can address it.

What if you do not have every kind of joint document?

Couples arrange their lives differently. You may rent a room with relatives, keep some finances separate or spend time apart for work. Write down the actual arrangement and collect the relevant records that exist. No single item in the table guarantees approval.

The USCIS Policy Manual lists several kinds of relationship evidence and allows other documentation showing that the marriage was genuine. It does not give a universal number of photographs or bank statements that makes every case sufficient.

If you use affidavits, follow the instructions for statements from at least two people who have known both spouses since conditional residence was granted and have personal knowledge of the relationship. They should explain what they know and how they know it, with their full name, address, date and place of birth, and any relationship to either spouse. The instructions call for original sworn or affirmed affidavits, supported by other types of evidence.

Do not ask someone to sign a generic story that is untrue. If the available evidence is thin or the marriage has ended, have the filing basis and evidence reviewed together.

Sort copies, translations and case-specific records

An orderly file helps you spot omissions before submission:

  1. Group records by subject and date. Keep shared-home records together and arrange financial records chronologically. A short contents list can help you review the file.
  2. Check readability. Make sure scans include the relevant pages and do not cut off names, dates or the reverse of a card.
  3. Follow the rules for originals. USCIS generally asks for legible copies, but some evidence, including affidavits and certain criminal records, has specific original or certification requirements. Check the instructions for each item.
  4. Add full English translations. Foreign-language documents need complete translations and the translator's certification of accuracy, completeness and competence, with the required identifying and contact information.
  5. Separate items needing advice. Flag arrests, charges, a divorce, a spouse's death, abuse, late filing or a child filing separately. Their documentation requirements are not covered by a shared-finances checklist.

Keep a complete copy of what you submit. The category headings and timeline suggested here are organizational choices, not an official packet format.

Before you submit

Review the whole filing, including the forms and supporting records:

  • Confirm the correct filing basis and deadline for your circumstances.
  • Check the current form edition, filing method, fee and submission instructions on the USCIS form page.
  • Check every required signature and any interpreter or preparer section.
  • Compare the addresses, employers and relationship dates in the form with your records.
  • Confirm that applicable card copies, translations and case-specific records are included.
  • Save the final submitted version and follow any USCIS notices you receive.

USCIS may request more evidence or an interview. An organized packet does not guarantee either approval or an interview waiver.

When to ask for a case review

Ask for a review before filing if you are unsure whether to file jointly, your spouse will not participate, your marriage has ended or your documents do not explain important parts of your history.
