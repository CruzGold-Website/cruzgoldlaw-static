Family-Based Immigration: Keeping Loved Ones Together

At Cruz Gold & Associates, we view every case file as a family story waiting for its next chapter.

The path to bringing your relatives to New Jersey depends entirely on two factors: your current legal status and your specific relationship to them. The system essentially splits family members into two distinct groups. Understanding exactly where your loved ones fit is the first step we take together.

Immediate Relatives: The Fastest Path

If you are a U.S. citizen, the law grants you specific privileges when petitioning for your closest family members.

Spouses, unmarried children under 21, and parents of U.S. citizens (provided you are at least 21 yourself) are classified as “Immediate Relatives.”

There is no annual cap on these visas.

What this means in practice: you do not have to wait for a visa number to become available. Once the petition is approved, the process moves directly to the next phase without an arbitrary delay. For many of the families we represent, this category is the most straightforward route to securing a Green Card.

The Preference Categories and the Waiting Game

Things get a bit more complicated when you move outside that immediate circle.

You might be wondering how long it takes to sponsor a family member in NJ who doesn’t fit the “immediate” definition. The reality is that it varies significantly. Siblings of U.S. citizens, married children, and adult children over 21 fall into “Family Preference” categories.

Unlike petitions for immediate relatives, these are subject to strict annual limits. And demand almost always exceeds supply.

Consequently, a backlog develops. Depending on your relative’s home country and their specific category, the wait can range from a few years to more than a decade. We help manage your expectations by monitoring the Visa Bulletin closely. It’s our job to ensure that when your priority date finally becomes current, your paperwork is ready to go immediately.

Understanding Your Rights as a Green Card Holder

A common question we encounter during consultations is whether a Green Card holder can sponsor their parents.

The answer is no.

As a Lawful Permanent Resident (LPR), you can petition for your spouse and your unmarried children. However, you cannot currently petition for your parents, married children, or siblings. To bring your parents to the United States, you must first become a U.S. citizen yourself. (We can help you navigate that naturalization process, too).

The Fiancé(e) Visa (K-1)

For those whose future spouses are currently abroad, the K-1 visa serves as a bridge to marriage in the United States.

This process allows your fiancé(e) to enter the U.S. for a 90-day window. During this time, you must get married. Once the marriage certificate is signed, we pivot immediately to the Adjustment of Status process to secure their permanent residency.

It is a time-sensitive path. But it’s also one that allows your life together to start on American soil sooner rather than later.

Adjustment of Status vs. Consular Processing

The physical location of your family member determines the legal procedure we use.

If your relative is already legally present in the United States – perhaps on a student or tourist visa – we typically file for an Adjustment of Status. This allows them to apply for their Green Card without having to leave the country.

But if they are still in their home country, we utilize Consular Processing. This involves working directly with the U.S. Department of State and the local U.S. consulate or embassy. We prepare clients extensively for these consular interviews because we know how nerve-wracking they can be.

Overcoming Obstacles: Waivers of Inadmissibility

Sometimes, the past complicates the future.

Previous unlawful presence in the U.S., criminal records, or prior violations can trigger a finding of “inadmissibility.” Initially, this sounds like a hard “no” from the government.

But it doesn’t always have to be the end of the road.

We routinely handle I-601 and I-601A waivers for clients facing these hurdles. These waivers essentially ask the government to “forgive” the inadmissibility grounds, usually by proving that denying the visa would cause extreme hardship to a qualifying U.S. relative. Proving “extreme hardship” requires a compassionate, detailed legal argument that goes far beyond standard forms.

It requires telling your family’s story with conviction.

We fight to keep families together because we believe you belong together. But sometimes, despite every legal effort to bring a family member here, the government initiates action to remove someone who is already established. When the system turns from administrative processing to aggressive enforcement, the nature of our representation shifts.

Pathways to Citizenship and Naturalization

For many of the families we work with, holding a Green Card feels like a massive weight has been lifted. It brings security. Relief.

But at Cruz Gold & Associates, we don’t look at residency as the finish line.

The true goal – the one that establishes your legacy in this country – is naturalization. Becoming a U.S. citizen builds a permanent shield around your status; you stop worrying about renewal dates or facing deportation laws that seem to shift with every election cycle. You gain a voice through the right to vote. You can travel freely with a U.S. passport. Perhaps most significantly, you gain far more power to petition for loved ones waiting to join you here.

Taking this final step usually involves navigating the N-400 application.

Clients often ask us when they can actually apply. The general rule requires five years of lawful permanent resident status. However, there is a notable exception. If you are married to a U.S. citizen and living with them, that timeline often drops to just three years.

Check the calendar, but don’t stop there.

Dates are only one part of the equation. You must also prove you have been physically present in the United States and demonstrate what the law terms “good moral character.” In practice, this means maintaining a clean record and meeting obligations like paying taxes or supporting dependents. We review your history with a fine-tooth comb (seriously, we check everything) to ensure no old mistakes surface to derail your application.

Then comes the interview.

We know the civics and English exams can cause sleepless nights. That nervousness is completely normal. We don’t just file your paperwork and send you off alone; we help you prepare.

Because we are a fully bilingual firm, we can break down complex history questions in Spanish first. We make sure you understand the concepts thoroughly before you practice answering them in English. We run through it with you. Again and again. Until you feel ready.

Sometimes, this journey isn’t even for you.

It might be for your children. Through the N-600 application, we help establish citizenship for kids who may have automatically acquired it through their parents. Securing that certificate acts as vital proof of their identity and rights.

The day you raise your right hand to take the Oath of Allegiance changes everything.

It is the specific moment you stop being a guest and become a fully recognized member of the American family. We have stood beside countless clients during this transformation, from that first worried consultation to the final, celebratory ceremony. It is exactly why we do what we do.

That said, we recognize that for some, the road is steeper. Not everyone has access to a straightforward path to residency due to past trauma or specific legal hurdles.
